Gumroad vs. Payhip: Which is Best for Beginners in 2026?

You finally did it. You spent the last three weeks staring at your computer screen, drinking too much coffee, and designing the perfect digital product. Maybe it is a beautifully organized Notion dashboard, a Canva eBook, or a custom website template.

You are incredibly proud of your work, and you are finally ready to share it with the world and start making a passive income.

But then, you hit a massive roadblock.

Where do you actually sell it? You type "best platforms to sell digital products" into Google, and you are immediately bombarded with dozens of options. Shopify wants $39 a month. Etsy wants a shop setup fee. Everyone else wants a piece of your profit.

As a beginner, paying expensive monthly subscription fees before you have even made your first sale is terrifying. It is the exact reason why so many talented creators give up before they even launch. You just want a simple, free platform where a customer can pay you, download their PDF, and leave.

If you are looking for zero monthly fees, your search has probably narrowed down to the two biggest heavyweights in the creator economy: Gumroad and Payhip.

Both platforms are entirely free to start. Both platforms allow you to sell digital files. But behind the scenes, their fees, their pay-out rules, and how they treat your customers are completely different.

I have personally built stores, launched products, and tested the checkout screens on both of these platforms. Today, I am going to give you the honest, unfiltered truth about how Gumroad and Payhip operate in 2026, so you can pick the exact right home for your digital business.

The Elephant in the Room: How the Fees Actually Work

When a platform says it is "Free to use," what they actually mean is that there are no monthly subscription bills. Instead, they take a small percentage of your money every time you make a sale.

If you are going to treat your digital products like a real business, you need to understand the math.

Gumroad’s Pricing Structure:

Gumroad used to have a tiered pricing system, but they recently changed to a flat rate to keep things simple. In 2026, Gumroad takes a flat 10% fee on every single sale you make.

On top of that 10%, you also have to pay standard credit card processing fees (which is usually another 2.9% plus 30 cents per transaction).

So, if you sell a website template for $10, Gumroad takes $1.00, the credit card fee takes about $0.59, and you are left with $8.41.

Payhip’s Pricing Structure:

Payhip is highly competitive when it comes to keeping your hard-earned money. On their "Free Forever" plan, Payhip only takes a 5% transaction fee.

Just like Gumroad, you still have to pay the standard PayPal or Stripe processing fees (2.9% + 30 cents), because those are charged by the banks, not the platform.

If you sell that same $10 template on Payhip, they take $0.50, the credit card fee takes $0.59, and you are left with $8.91.

The Winner for Fees: Payhip. Keeping an extra 5% of your total revenue might not seem like a big deal on day one, but when your business grows and you are making $1,000 a month, that is an extra $50 staying in your pocket. Payhip even offers a paid monthly plan where they drop their transaction fee to 0%, which is a great option for when your store gets huge.

Getting Paid: The Minimum Threshold Trap

Making a sale on your phone feels amazing. But actually getting that money transferred into your real-life bank account is a completely different story. This is where a lot of international creators get a very nasty surprise.

How Gumroad Pays You?

Gumroad holds your money in a digital vault. They process payouts once a week (usually on Fridays). However, if you live outside of the United States, UK, or Australia—for example, if you are a creator based in India running your business through PayPal—Gumroad currently enforces a strict $100 minimum payout threshold.

This means if you are selling a $5 Notion template, you have to wait until you make 20 sales before Gumroad will finally release the money to your bank account. If you need that cash to pay for your own business expenses right now, this waiting period can be incredibly frustrating.

How Payhip Pays You?

Payhip does not hold your money. They don't even have a digital vault.

When you set up a Payhip store, you connect it directly to your own personal PayPal or Stripe account. When a customer buys your product, Payhip instantly calculates their 5% fee, takes it, and routes the rest of the money directly into your PayPal account that exact same second.

There are zero minimum thresholds. If you make a $3 sale today, you have $3 in your PayPal account today.

The Winner for Payouts: Payhip. Instant access to your cash flow is a massive advantage for new entrepreneurs.

The Hidden Superpower: Gumroad Discover

So far, it sounds like Payhip is beating Gumroad in almost every category. Why do millions of creators still use Gumroad?

When you build a store on Payhip, it acts as a standalone island on the internet. If you do not actively post your Payhip link on Pinterest, YouTube, or Threads, you will get exactly zero visitors. You have to bring 100% of your own traffic.

Gumroad, on the other hand, is a digital shopping mall. They have a feature called "Gumroad Discover." Millions of people go to Gumroad.com every month and use the search bar to look for things like "Aesthetic Notion Planner" or "Procreate Brushes."
If you use the right SEO tags on your product listing, Gumroad will actually recommend your product to strangers.

If Gumroad finds a customer for you through their Discover search engine, they will take 30% fee on that specific sale. That sounds like a lot, but you have to remember: you did zero marketing for that sale. You are essentially paying Gumroad a finder's fee for bringing you a customer you never would have found on your own.
The Winner for Traffic: Gumroad.

Having a built-in search engine that pushes your products while you sleep is an incredible asset for beginners who don't have a big social media following yet.

Taxes and The "Merchant of Record" Advantage

If there is one thing that scares new creators more than marketing, it is international taxes.

If a customer from Germany buys your eBook, European law states that you technically owe Value Added Tax (VAT) to the German government. Trying to calculate, collect, and file taxes for 50 different countries is a legal nightmare.

Thankfully, you don't have to.

Both Gumroad and Payhip act as the "Merchant of Record." In the eyes of international law, the transaction doesn't happen between you and the customer. Gumroad (or Payhip) officially buys the product from you, and then they officially sell it to the customer.

Because they act as the legal middleman, they calculate the correct tax, add it to the customer's bill at checkout, collect the money, and send it to the foreign governments for you. You never have to touch a single international tax form. This feature alone makes both of these platforms worth their fees.

The Final Verdict: Which One Should You Choose?

There is no single "perfect" platform. The right choice completely depends on your current marketing strategy and where you live.

You should choose Gumroad if:

  • You do not have a large social media following yet and want to rely on the "Gumroad Discover" search engine to help you find buyers.
  • You don't mind waiting to reach the $100 minimum payout threshold (if you are outside standard direct-deposit countries).
  • You want the absolute most recognized, trusted checkout name in the creator economy.

You should choose Payhip if:

  • You are already getting your own traffic from Pinterest, Medium, or YouTube, and just need a reliable place to host your files.
  • You want the absolute lowest fees possible so you can keep more of your profit.
  • You want your money instantly deposited into your PayPal account the exact second you make a sale.
  • You want a completely flawless, instant-download experience for your customers.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can I sell physical products on these platforms?

Both Gumroad and Payhip allow you to sell physical products (like t-shirts or printed books). They let you collect shipping addresses and charge shipping fees. However, their true strength is in digital downloads. If your main business is physical shipping, a platform like Shopify might serve you better in the long run.

Do I need a registered business or LLC to use them?

No! You do not need to be a registered corporation to start selling. Both platforms allow you to sign up as an "Individual" or "Sole Proprietor." You just need to provide your basic tax ID (like a Social Security Number or PAN card) and a bank account to verify your identity.

Can I give away products for free to build an email list?

Yes, this is one of the best features of both platforms. You can set the price of your product to "$0" or use a "Pay What You Want" model. Customers can enter their email address to get the file for free, and both platforms will save those emails so you can send them newsletters later.

How do I stop people from stealing my PDF files?

Digital piracy is a reality of the internet, but both Gumroad and Payhip have a built-in feature to protect your work called "PDF Stamping." If you turn this feature on, the platform will automatically generate a subtle watermark at the top of your PDF document containing the buyer's email address. People are much less likely to upload your file to a public forum if their personal email address is stamped on every page!

Can I connect my own custom domain?

Yes! If you don't want your store to say yourname.gumroad.com, both platforms allow you to connect a custom .com domain. However, a cheaper and cleaner method is to build a beautiful, free landing page on Google Sites or Publii, and simply link your "Buy Now" buttons to your checkout cart!
