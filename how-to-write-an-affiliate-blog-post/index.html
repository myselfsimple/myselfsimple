How to write an affiliate blog post Using AI (10 Exact Best Prompts)

Introduction

Let me be completely honest with you. When I first started out my blog, figuring out how to write an affiliate blog post that actually converted readers into buyers, I felt impossible. I would literally stare at a blank Google Doc for hours, and trying to review tech products like web hosting without any proper knowledge.

I’d have two tabs open: one with Hostinger’s pricing page, and another with GoDaddy’s feature list. I was trying to figure out how I was going to make things like “NVMe SSD Storage,” “LiteSpeed web servers,” and “99.9% uptime guarantees” sound exciting to a beginner trying to start their first blog.

It was exhausting. Helpful review takes hours of research. We all know that web hosting is one of the most profitable affiliate niches out there, but the barrier to entry is the content itself. How do you write a 2,000-word review without losing your mind?

Enter Artificial Intelligence. And I searched “how to write an affiliate blog post.”

How to Write an Affiliate Blog Post

Over the last few months here at Simple Site Steps, I’ve completely overhauled my writing process. I don’t write from scratch anymore. Instead, I use AI as my personal research assistant, outlining expert, and copywriter. But here is the catch: I don’t just click a button and let AI write the whole thing. Google’s algorithms are smarter than that. To rank on page one, we need to insert our own human Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T).

Today, I’m pulling back the curtain. I’m going to share the exact 10 AI prompts I use to write high-converting web hosting affiliate reviews in record time, while keeping that crucial human touch.

Let’s start.

Why You Shouldn’t Just Ask AI to “Write a Review”

Before we get to the prompts, we need to have a quick heart-to-heart about SEO.

If you go into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and type: “Write a 1000-word affiliate review of Hostinger Web Hosting,” you are going to get a robotic, generic, and painfully boring article. It will start with “In today’s fast-paced digital world…” (Ugh, please, no).

More importantly, Google hates this. They want to see first-hand experience. They want to know that you actually understand what makes a good web host.

So, how do we use AI effectively? We break the review down into simple site steps. We use AI to do the heavy lifting—researching features, structuring the article, and translating technical jargon—so that we can focus on adding our personal opinions, screenshots, and real-world results.

Here are the 10 prompts I use to build the perfect affiliate review, piece by piece.

The 10 Exact Prompts for Web Hosting Reviews

Prompt 1: The “Heavy Lifter” Research Prompt

When I start a comparison review, the first thing I need is the raw data. I don’t want to hunt through five different web pages to find out how much RAM a starter plan has for my “how to write an affiliate blog post” article. I let AI do the data scraping for me.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"I am writing an affiliate review comparing [Host A] and [Host B] for my blog targeted at total beginners. Act as a senior web developer and build a detailed comparison table. Compare their cheapest shared hosting plans based on: Price (first year vs renewal), storage limits, bandwidth, free domains, SSL certificates, customer support options, and server speed technology. Present this in a clean, easy-to-read table."

Why this works: It instantly organizes the facts. Instead of writing, you are now just fact-checking. You can take this table, drop it straight into your blog post, and instantly provide massive value to your reader who just wants a quick comparison.

Prompt 2: The SEO-Optimized Outline Generator

A good review needs structure. Don’t just write how to write an affiliate blog post only using paragraphs. If your blog post is just a wall of text, your readers will bounce, and Google will drop your rankings. We need clear H2 and H3 headings.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"Act as an expert SEO content strategist. Create a comprehensive blog post outline for a review of [Web Host Name]. The target audience is beginner bloggers who are non-technical. Include engaging H2 and H3 headings that target long-tail keywords. Make sure the structure flows logically from introduction to pricing, performance, pros/cons, and a final verdict."

Why this works: When I use this prompt, AI gives me a beautiful skeleton for my article. It usually suggests sections I wouldn’t have thought of, like “What Happens When Your Traffic Spikes on [Host Name]?” which is a highly searched question!

Prompt 3: The Feature-to-Benefit Translator (The Money Prompt)

If there is one prompt you take away from this post, make it this one. This is how we make money as affiliates. Beginners do not care about “object caching” or “PHP 8.2”. They care about what those features do for them. We need to translate features into benefits for our blog “how to write an affiliate blog post”.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"I am reviewing [Web Host Name]. They offer the following technical features: [Paste a list of features, e.g., NVMe SSD storage, CDN integration, LiteSpeed servers]. As an expert copywriter, translate each of these technical features into a beginner-friendly benefit. Explain how it will actually help a new blogger get more traffic, save time, or make money, using conversational language."

Why this works: AI will take “NVMe SSD Storage” and turn it into: “Lightning-Fast Loading Speeds: This host uses top-tier NVMe storage, which is a fancy way of saying your blog will load in under a second. This keeps your readers from getting impatient and leaving, meaning more page views and more affiliate sales for you.” That is pure gold.

Prompt 4: The PAS Introduction Hook

The first 100 words of your blog post dictate whether someone stays or leaves. I always use the PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution) formula to hook my readers to my blog (how to write an affiliate blog post).

Copy and paste this prompt:

"Write a 150-word introduction for my review of [Web Host Name]. Use the PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution) copywriting framework. The problem is feeling overwhelmed by all the hosting choices. The agitation is the fear of picking a slow host and losing blog traffic. The solution is my honest, hype-free review of [Web Host Name]. Write in the first person ('I' and 'we'), keep the tone friendly and empathetic, and do not use the phrase 'in today's digital world'."

Why this works: It creates immediate empathy. When your reader feels like you understand their exact struggle, they trust you. And in the affiliate marketing game, trust equals conversions.

Prompt 5: The “Skimmer-Friendly” Pros and Cons

Let’s face it: people skim online. When I land on a review, I immediately scroll down to look for the Pros and Cons box.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"Based on the current web hosting market, list the 5 biggest pros and 3 biggest cons of using [Web Host Name] for a brand new WordPress blog. Be brutally honest. Frame the cons as minor warnings rather than deal breakers. Output this as a bulleted list that I can put in a summary box at the top of my article."

Why this works: By asking the AI to be “brutally honest” and framing the cons as warnings, you dramatically boost your E-E-A-T score. A review that only says positive things looks like a scam. Sharing a minor negative (e.g., “Customer support wait times can be slightly longer on weekends”) makes your glowing recommendation much more believable.

Prompt 6: The “People Also Ask” FAQ Section

If you want to rank for long-tail keywords and snatch those rich snippets on Google, you absolutely must have an FAQ section at the bottom of your post (how to write an affiliate blog post).

Copy and paste this prompt:

"What are the 5 most common questions beginners ask on Google and Reddit before buying hosting from [Web Host Name]? Write a short, helpful, 2-to-3 sentence answer for each question. Keep the tone reassuring and direct."

Why this works: Google loves FAQ schema. By answering the exact questions people are typing into the search bar, you increase your chances of showing up in the “People Also Ask” drop-downs on the search engine results page.

Prompt 7: The Pricing Tier Demystifier (Exposing Hidden Fees)

Web hosting pricing is notoriously confusing. Hosts love to advertise “$2.95/month,” but beginners don’t realize they have to pay for 3 years upfront, or that it renews at $14.99/month. If I want my readers to trust me, I have to be completely transparent about pricing. This prompt does the math for me.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"I am reviewing [Web Host Name]. They have three main shared hosting tiers: [List Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3]. Act as a transparent, consumer-advocate affiliate marketer. Break down these three pricing tiers for a beginner. Highlight the introductory price versus the renewal price. Tell the reader exactly which tier is the 'Best Value' for a brand new blog, and explicitly warn them about any up-sells they should uncheck at checkout."

Why this works: Nothing builds trust faster than saving your reader money. When you actively warn a beginner not to buy the unnecessary “Site-lock Security” up-sell, or explain the renewal shock, you stop sounding like a sleazy salesman and start sounding like a trusted mentor. This skyrockets your Trustworthiness (the “T” in E-E-A-T).

Prompt 8: The “Best Alternatives” Generator

Here is a secret I learned the hard way: not every reader is going to want the host you are reviewing. If they decide it’s not for them, and you don’t offer alternatives, they will hit the “back” button and buy from a competitor’s website. We want to keep them on Simple Site Steps!

Copy and paste this prompt:

"I have just written a review for [Web Host Name]. Some readers will realize this host is not right for them. Suggest 3 alternative web hosts. For Alternative 1, provide a better option for absolute budget seekers. For Alternative 2, provide a better option for premium speed and customer service. For Alternative 3, provide a better option for scaling a high-traffic site. Write a 2-sentence summary for each, explaining exactly why someone should choose it over [Web Host Name]."

Why this works: It captures the “on-the-fence” buyers. You can easily hyperlink those three alternatives to your other affiliate reviews or directly to your affiliate links. Google also loves this because it provides a comprehensive, unbiased view of the market, proving your Expertise.

Prompt 9: The “Real-World Use Case” Scenarios

Technical specs don’t sell web hosting; visualization does. A beginner isn’t asking, “Can this host run PHP 8?” They are asking, “Can this host run my food blog?” I use this prompt to help readers visualize their own success.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"I want to help my readers visualize using [Web Host Name]. Create 3 highly relatable, real-world 'use case' scenarios for beginners. For example: A stay-at-home mom starting a recipe blog, a freelancer building a graphic design portfolio, and a hobbyist starting a local podcast. Explain briefly how [Web Host Name]'s specific features (like 1-click WordPress install or unmetered bandwidth) make it the perfect fit for these specific types of websites. Keep the tone encouraging and inspiring."

Why this works: It bridges the gap between cold tech and human emotion. When a reader sees their specific dream (like starting a freelance portfolio) mentioned in your review, they immediately feel understood. It dramatically increases the chances of them clicking your affiliate link because they now believe the product was made specifically for them.

Prompt 10: The High-Converting Verdict & Call to Action (CTA)

Finally, we need to tell the reader exactly what to do next. A weak conclusion will cost you commissions.

Copy and paste this prompt:

"Write a concluding 'Final Verdict' section for my review of [Web Host Name]. Summarize who this host is perfect for (e.g., budget-conscious beginners) and who should avoid it (e.g., massive e-commerce stores). End with a strong, urgent Call to Action encouraging them to click my affiliate link to get a special discount and start their blog today."

Why this works: It removes the friction of decision-making. You are explicitly telling the reader: If you fit this profile, buy this right now.

The Golden Rule: Adding Your “E-E-A-T” (The Human Touch)

Do not just paste these AI outputs into WordPress and hit publish.

If you want to survive Google’s algorithm updates, you have to prove that a human being is behind the keyboard. Here is how I add E-E-A-T to my AI-assisted drafts before I publish them on Simple Site Steps:

  • I insert my own screenshots: If I am reviewing Hostinger, I log into my actual Hostinger dashboard, take a screenshot of the control panel, draw a red arrow on it, and upload it. This proves I actually use the product.
  • I add a “My Personal Experience” box: I write a 100-word paragraph completely from my own brain. I talk about my experience talking to their live chat support, or how easy it was to install WordPress.
  • I format relentlessly: I use bold text, italicized words, and custom graphics. I make the post visually beautiful. AI can give you words, but it can’t give you web design (yet).

Final Thoughts: Taking Your First Simple Site Step

Writing web hosting affiliate reviews used to feel like climbing a mountain. Today, with these 10 AI prompts, it feels like a walk in the park.

By combining the raw processing power of AI with your own unique human experiences and opinions, you can pump out highly helpful, SEO-optimized reviews that genuinely assist beginners—and earn you a great income in the process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will Google penalize AI-generated web hosting reviews?
No, Google has explicitly stated that they do not penalize content simply because it is generated by AI. However, Google’s algorithms do penalize low-quality, unedited content. As long as you use AI prompts to assist your writing and inject your own personal experience, screenshots, and opinions (E-E-A-T), your web hosting reviews can safely rank on page one.
Can I use the free version of ChatGPT to write affiliate reviews?
Yes, you can absolutely use the free version of ChatGPT (or Claude and Gemini) to write your content. The 10 AI prompts for web hosting reviews shared in this guide are designed to work perfectly on any free AI model. However, paid versions often have live web-browsing capabilities, which makes pulling up-to-date pricing for hosting companies much faster.
Will these AI prompts pass AI detection tools?
While these specific prompts are designed to create conversational and human-sounding text, raw AI output might still be flagged by AI detectors. But don’t worry—Google doesn’t use standard AI detectors to rank content. Instead of worrying about passing a detector, focus on editing the text to add your unique voice and real-world expertise.
What is the best AI tool for blogging and affiliate marketing?
For structural outlining and conversational tone, ChatGPT and Claude are currently the industry favorites for bloggers. If you need an AI tool that seamlessly integrates live Google search data to check web host server specs, Google’s Gemini is a fantastic free alternative.
Can I use these exact prompts for other affiliate niches?
Absolutely. While these templates are optimized as AI prompts for web hosting reviews, you can easily swap out the variables. For example, you can replace “shared hosting plans” with “email marketing software” or “WordPress themes” and the AI will generate the same high-quality research and outlines for any affiliate niche.
